# When you need help

[العربية](../ar/getting-help.md)

Start by deciding what you're seeing: routine updates, an unrecognized account/device, an active takeover, or a possible targeted attack. The response may differ.

[EFF's security planning guide](https://ssd.eff.org/module/your-security-plan) helps you identify the data and people you need to protect, the threats you face, and practical tradeoffs.

## Possible account takeover

Use a trusted device to open the provider's official security/recovery page. Review its recovery instructions and recognized sign-in methods. Don't follow a security-alert link just because a message looks urgent.

If an unfamiliar device or app really has access, plan revocation and credential recovery with the provider. Preserve a working recovery path. An agent can explain the steps; you should handle passwords, authentication enrollment and recovery codes privately.

## Possible targeted compromise

For an activist, journalist, human-rights defender or civil-society organization, [Access Now's Digital Security Helpline](https://www.accessnow.org/help/) offers specialist assistance. Review its eligibility and current contact instructions. This guide doesn't contact anyone or send your reports.

If you received an Apple threat notification, verify it through the account website and follow [Apple's response guidance](https://support.apple.com/en-us/102174). Consider its recommendation for specialist help. [Lockdown Mode](https://support.apple.com/en-us/105120) is an option with restrictions that deserve a deliberate decision.

Avoid destroying evidence with a wipe, bulk cleanup, browser reset, mass token removal or a collection of unknown scanners before discussing preservation with an appropriate expert. If activity appears ongoing, get advice from a separate trusted device about containment and preserving evidence.

## What an audit can tell you

| Finding | Reasonable conclusion |
|---|---|
| A security setting is enabled | That specific protection was verified at that time. |
| A scanner found no known indicator | It didn't find the indicators within its documented search scope. |
| A device location looks unfamiliar | Check travel, VPN use, device ownership and activity before drawing conclusions. |
| A program has a valid publisher signature | Its signature/integrity check passed; that doesn't establish harmless behavior. |
| A port is listening | Identify the owner and reachability. The number doesn't identify an attacker. |

Keep evidence and personal details private. Don't post account pages, session links, serial numbers, full logs or recovery material in this repository's public issues.

**Finished:** the immediate concern has an appropriate response path, and you understand what remains uncertain.
