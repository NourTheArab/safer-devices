# Phones matter too

[العربية](../ar/phones.md)

Your phone may hold your email, authenticator, messages, passkeys and account-recovery access. Include it in the plan for your laptop.

## Start with these checks

- Use a device passcode you don't share. Review biometric access and the lock-screen information visible to someone holding the phone.
- Check system updates and app updates. On Android, check the security-patch and Google Play system-update status as well as the Android version. [Google's update guide](https://support.google.com/android/answer/7680439) explains these separate fields.
- Confirm your important accounts have the protection and recovery options you intend. Follow the [account guide](accounts.md).
- Check that a recent backup exists, and understand how you would restore it if the phone were lost. Protect the account holding that backup.
- Review app access to location, microphone, camera, contacts and photos. Keep permissions you need for accessibility and trusted everyday features.

On an iPhone, use [Apple's update instructions](https://support.apple.com/en-us/118575). On Android, follow your phone manufacturer's guidance as well as Google's; available controls and support lifetimes differ.

## Protect recovery before changing things

Removing an old phone from an account can also remove a trusted authentication device. Check that another working method and a private recovery copy exist first. Don't reset an authenticator or uninstall a password manager or wallet as routine cleanup.

Let the owner enter passcodes, passwords, enrollment codes and recovery material. An agent can help interpret a setting without collecting them.

## If your work could make you a target

Discuss the risk with a specialist rather than adding many scanners. [Apple's Lockdown Mode](https://support.apple.com/en-us/105120) provides additional restrictions for people facing sophisticated targeted attacks, with feature tradeoffs. Review those before enabling it.

Verify an alleged Apple threat notification by signing directly into the Apple Account website. [Apple explains genuine notifications](https://support.apple.com/en-us/102174) and recommends expert assistance. [Access Now](https://www.accessnow.org/help/) supports civil society and people targeted because of their work.

**Finished:** your phone is included in updates, backups, recovery and account checks. A laptop audit alone doesn't cover it.
